# Egyptian Arabic Receipt Extraction — Corrected August 2026 Comparison

**Prepared:** 31 August 2026. **Scope:** one 1–2 MP Egyptian supermarket thermal-receipt image, Arabic RTL columns, approximately 400 output tokens, plain HTTPS from Node/Hono, and a current hard budget of $5/month for roughly 1–5 receipts/day.

## Executive recommendation

The strongest **Chinese/API candidate** is **Alibaba Cloud Model Studio’s international Qwen-OCR endpoint**. It is the only Chinese commercial option in this review for which I verified all of the following from current provider-owned pages: a receipt/table OCR use case, an international deployment scope, a current public price, a free-quota condition, and an explicit no-training statement. Its documented Qwen3-VL OCR capability page also explicitly names **Arabic, RTL scripts, receipts/invoices, tables, low light, blur, tilt, and layout understanding**. These remain vendor claims, not an independent Egyptian-receipt benchmark, so run a 100–200 receipt pilot before production.

The best **privacy-first route** is **self-hosted DeepSeek-OCR** or **self-hosted Qwen3-VL**, but neither should be treated as a complete receipt parser until you test item–price pairing and add deterministic reconciliation. The best **commercial semantic parser overall** remains Google Document AI Expense Parser, but at the user’s stated 150 receipts/month it costs approximately $15/month and therefore fails the $5 ceiling. The best cost-constrained general-model fallback is a current low-cost vision model with strict schema validation, not a free tier.

I am **not recommending Gemini** in this corrected report. If a report authored by an AI company recommends that company’s own model as “unequivocal,” that relationship must be disclosed as a potential conflict. The independent evidence cited here is useful, but the recommendation below is based on the user’s data terms, Arabic/RTL claims, receipt/table fit, current price, and deployability—not on the author’s corporate affiliation.

## Cost model

Because each vendor tokenizes a 1–2 MP image differently, no honest cross-provider comparison can claim one universal exact image-token count. The normalized calculation below uses **2,000 billed input image/text tokens plus 400 output tokens**. Where a provider gives an image-dimension conversion rule, production code must call that provider’s estimator or inspect actual usage. At five receipts/day for 30 days, the reference volume is **150 receipts/month** and the budget allowance is **$0.0333/receipt**.

| Current model/path | Current public rate retrieved 31 Aug 2026 | One-receipt arithmetic under 2,000 + 400 assumption | Cost per receipt |
|---|---|---|---:|
| **Alibaba Qwen-OCR** (`qwen-vl-ocr`, international) | $0.07/M input, $0.16/M output; Alibaba pricing page updated 2026-08-31 | `(2,000 × 0.07 / 1,000,000) + (400 × 0.16 / 1,000,000)` | **$0.000204** |
| **DeepSeek V4 Flash Vision Exp**, off-peak | $0.22/M input, $0.66/M output; off-peak; image tokens are dimension-derived | `(2,000 × 0.22 / 1,000,000) + (400 × 0.66 / 1,000,000)` | **$0.000704** |
| **GLM-OCR** | CNY 0.20/M input and CNY 0.20/M output; Zhipu page retrieved 2026-08-31 | `(2,000 + 400) × 0.20 / 1,000,000` | **CNY 0.00048**; USD conversion depends on FX |
| Google Document AI Expense Parser | $0.10/count; one image is one page and one count covers up to 10 pages | `1 × $0.10` | **$0.10** |

The first three are **normalized token arithmetic**, not a promise that the image will tokenize to exactly 2,000 tokens. For DeepSeek, the official pricing page explicitly says images are converted into tokens by dimensions and billed with text tokens. For GLM-OCR, the provider publishes CNY pricing rather than a USD rate. Google’s Expense Parser price is page/count based and is therefore exact for a one-image receipt under its published billing rule. [1] [2] [3] [4]

### What “verified” means here

For a field marked **verified**, I found the claim on a provider-owned page retrieved on or before 31 August 2026. For a field marked **not published**, I name the pages checked above and do not fill the gap with a reseller claim, a search snippet, or an assumption based on the provider’s nationality. The Chinese section is therefore more complete than the earlier reports even where the answer is that the provider did not publish the requested term.

## Arabic and table-structure evidence

The best independent benchmark located is **KITAB-Bench**, an Arabic OCR/document-understanding benchmark covering layout detection, table recognition, charts, and multiple document domains. Its published findings report that GPT-4o, Gemini, and Qwen outperform traditional OCR baselines on the benchmark. That is relevant evidence for Arabic document understanding, but KITAB-Bench is not an Egyptian supermarket thermal-receipt benchmark and does not prove correct item-to-price pairing in an RTL retail table. [15]

A newer multilingual document-parsing line of work, **MDPBench/MORE**, is relevant to table and structure parsing, and search results indicate that Qwen3-VL and PaddleOCR-VL are evaluated on multilingual photographed documents. However, I did not find a published result specifically measuring Egyptian Arabic thermal receipts, dialect normalization, or the exact `raw_name → canonical_name → qty → unit_price → line_total` schema. The correct conclusion is not that these models fail; it is that the user’s decisive metric remains unmeasured publicly.

The acceptance test should score four separate things: Arabic character transcription, row/column association, arithmetic reconciliation, and semantic normalization. A system may read `بندورة` perfectly while attaching the price from the next RTL column or outputting a grand total that does not equal the line totals.

## Final decision

**Choose Alibaba Qwen-OCR international for the first commercial pilot**, not because it is Chinese, but because its current official materials provide the best combined evidence for this exact image problem: Arabic/RTL, receipts, tables, layout order, tilt, blur, and low light, plus a current international price and an explicit no-training statement. Before production, obtain written confirmation of the contracting entity, selected processing region, retention/deletion period, human-review policy, subprocessor list, and cross-border transfer mechanism. The international deployment must not be conflated with China/Beijing deployment.

**Named second choice: self-hosted Qwen3-VL**, if the household-data requirement is more important than operational simplicity. It removes provider retention and residency risk, but it adds GPU and engineering cost and still requires a receipt-specific benchmark. If self-hosting is too heavy, use **DeepSeek-OCR** as the privacy-first OCR front end, but do not claim Arabic support until your own test confirms it.

**Commercial semantic fallback:** Google Document AI Expense Parser if the budget can rise from $5 to approximately $15/month at 150 receipts, or Azure Receipt if Microsoft confirms a lower current regional price and Arabic support in the exact prebuilt Receipt model.

## Required pilot gates

Use 100–200 real receipts covering at least five stores, glare, folds, angled photos, Arabic-Indic and Western numerals, weighted produce, promotions, missing branches, handwritten marks, and dialect variants such as `بندورة → طماطم`. Require exact JSON Schema validation, line-total arithmetic reconciliation, a confidence/abstention path, and manual review for any mismatch. Do not let the model silently “repair” a total without preserving the raw OCR evidence.
